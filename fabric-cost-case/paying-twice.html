02The estateWhat we are actually moving

This is not a big-data problem. It is a reconciliation problem.

Sustainability and enterprise data management for a group like this means pulling twelve systems into one auditable ledger, then defending every number to an assurance provider.

The volumes are modest. The lineage requirements are brutal. A single Scope 3 Category 6 figure has to trace back through a Concur expense line, an Amex statement row, a distance-based emission factor version, and a currency conversion — and survive a question about it eighteen months later.

04The diagnosisWhere the money goes

Half of it is serving dashboards.

Databricks prices elastic distributed compute. An ESG estate is small, bursty and governance-heavy. The mismatch shows up in one line.

Why serving dominates

  • A SQL warehouse has to be warm to feel fast. Auto-stop at ten minutes still leaves it billing through the working day.
  • Power BI import refreshes hammer the warehouse on a schedule, then Power BI stores a second copy of the same data in a semantic model.
  • Concurrency from 350 consumers at month-end forces a size-up that runs all month.
  • Dev and QA each carry their own warehouse so report developers can test — $792 a month to serve nobody.

What is genuinely Databricks-shaped

  • Batch ELT and interactive development — $4,220 a month, 45% of compute. Real work, fairly priced, and portable.
  • Nothing in this estate needs sub-second streaming, a GPU, or a distributed training run.
  • The largest single table is 240 GB. A single node handles most of the silver layer.
The finding

The estate pays a distributed-compute premium for a workload whose dominant cost is keeping a query engine warm for BI — on a tenant that already owns a query engine kept warm for BI.

05Why nowThe committed capacity

The second platform is already bought, and it is two-thirds idle.

An F64 reservation costs roughly $5,003 a month — $60,036 a year — whether it runs one report or the whole estate. On a Power BI-only tenant it typically sits at 35–45% capacity-unit utilisation.

That is the whole argument. On committed capacity, the marginal cost of a workload is zero until you saturate the SKU. Every batch job moved onto headroom is a job that stops generating a Databricks invoice and starts generating nothing.

Two things that are free, and shouldn’t be

An F64 reservation includes 64 TB of mirroring storage — one free terabyte per capacity unit — against a 1.8 TB estate. And at F64 and above, holders of a free Fabric licence with a viewer role can read Power BI content, so the ~350 report consumers need no Pro seats. Both are already paid for and neither is currently being used.

What disappears

  • Two of the three ADLS copies, and the sync jobs that kept them consistent.
  • The second security model, and the drift between them.

What carries over almost unchanged

  • PySpark notebooks. The estate is Delta-on-Parquet either side; most transforms port with a path change and a session-config edit.
  • The medallion layout, the ESG data model, and every business rule in it.
  • Git-backed CI/CD — Fabric deployment pipelines replace the Terraform-plus-notebook-sync arrangement.
  • The Delta tables themselves, read in place through shortcuts during the whole transition.

09The plan40 weeks, three decision gates

Nothing is switched off until its replacement has reconciled.

The first move is a mirror, not a migration. Unity Catalog tables appear in OneLake read-only with no data copied and no Databricks change — so phase one is reversible by deleting an item.

WK 0–6

Discovery & mirror

Stand up a Mirrored Azure Databricks Catalog. The whole estate becomes readable in OneLake with nothing copied. Port one complete ESG domain — Scope 2 from metered utilities plus Concur travel — and measure its real capacity-unit draw against the live F64.

WK 4–16

Ingestion

SAP, Concur and Amex extraction moves to Fabric Data Factory and mirroring. Azure Data Factory runs in parallel and is retired only once row counts and control totals match for two consecutive monthly closes.

WK 12–24

Transforms

Silver and gold notebooks port to Fabric Spark. Emission-factor versioning and restatement logic — the hardest part of the estate — is ported first and reconciled against Databricks output to the tCO₂e, not to the row count.

WK 20–30

Serving

Semantic models rebuilt on Direct Lake. Reports repointed one workspace at a time. The DBSQL warehouses stay up through this phase — this is where the parallel-run cost sits, and it is deliberate.

WK 28–40

Decommission

Dev and QA workspaces first — that is $36,588 a year off the bill with zero production exposure. Production last, after a full quarter-end close runs clean on Fabric, with a 30-day rollback window during which the Databricks workspace stays provisioned but idle.

10PaybackCumulative net position

On technology alone this is a patient investment, and one case never clears.

A $233,000 one-time programme — $185,000 of engineering and partner time, $48,000 of parallel run — lands before a single saving does. Against a platform-only saving, it takes years to earn back.

The one thing that changes this materially

Stripped of headcount, the migration programme is larger than a single year of savings — so timing is no longer an optimisation, it is the deal. If the Databricks commitment renews inside the next twelve months, cut over at the renewal date: you stop paying the old platform on the day you would otherwise have re-signed, and most of the $48,000 parallel run disappears with it. Start this mid-commit and you are funding two platforms to reach a break-even four years out.

11The dividendWhy one layer compounds

Every AI capability arriving in the next three years lands on the storage layer, not the compute layer.

The cost case pays for the move. This is the part that makes it worth doing even if the cost case were neutral.

An agent is only as good as the semantic layer beneath it. Today a question like “why did Category 6 rise 14% in Q3?” has two possible answers in this estate, because the gold table in Databricks and the semantic model in Power BI are separately maintained. On one layer, there is one answer, and it is the same one the auditor sees.

One grounding storeOneLake is where the agent looks. No vector-store sprawl, no export-to-index pipeline, no third copy of commercially sensitive supplier data sitting in a side system nobody governs.
Fabric data agentsNatural-language questions answered against the same tables and the same measures that produce the disclosure. A site manager asks about their own consumption; row-level security has already decided what “their own” means.
AI functions in the pipelineClassifying 40,000 Amex merchant-coded transactions to emission-factor categories is a text-classification job, not an engineering job. It runs inline in a notebook and bills to the same capacity units as everything else.
Copilot, everywhere, includedIn notebooks, in Data Factory, in DAX, in the warehouse. No separate seat, no separate contract, no separate approval cycle.
Lineage that survives the questionPurview lineage and sensitivity labels flow end to end. An assurance provider tracing a tCO₂e figure back to a Concur line item follows one graph instead of two plus a spreadsheet.
Sustainability data solutionsA prebuilt ESG data model with 23 quantitative CSRD metrics, generally available in Fabric and mapped to ESRS and GRI disclosure points. This exists on one side of the comparison and not the other — and it is the single most expensive thing this team would otherwise build by hand.
The compounding argument

Every capability above gets better when there is more data on the layer, and none of them care which compute engine wrote it. Splitting the estate across two platforms means choosing, for each new capability, which half of your data it can see. Consolidating means never having that conversation again.

12The other sideWhere this argument fails

Five ways this goes wrong, and what to do about each.

If a deck like this has no honest counter-case, it is marketing. These are the real ones.

01

Capacity throttling is a real operational risk

Fabric smooths background work and bursts interactive work, but a saturated capacity throttles — and throttling hits report users, not just the batch job that caused it. Databricks solves this by spinning up another cluster and sending you the bill. Mitigation: surge protection and workspace-level capacity limits configured before cutover, ELT scheduled into the overnight window, and Gate 1 exists specifically to measure real capacity-unit draw before committing to a SKU size.

02

The shortcut governance gap is not theoretical

When Fabric reads Unity Catalog tables through shortcuts, Unity Catalog’s access policies do not apply to Fabric users — access is brokered by the identity that configured the connection. Microsoft’s own documentation calls out the potential for information-security policy violations. Mitigation: re-express every Unity Catalog grant as a OneLake data access role during phase one, driven off the same Entra groups, and treat the mapping as a deliverable with sign-off, not a configuration detail.

03

Keep Databricks if any of these are true

A production MLflow model registry with real governance around it. Sub-minute streaming at genuine scale. A multi-cloud mandate that makes Azure-only a board-level problem. A large, deeply tuned Scala codebase. None of these are true of this estate — but if any becomes true, the answer is a small Databricks footprint alongside Fabric, reading OneLake through shortcuts, not a reversal.

04

Spark runtime parity has to be verified, not assumed

Fabric’s Spark runtime trails Databricks Runtime, and Delta Live Tables has no like-for-like equivalent. Mitigation: a feature audit in week one, before any commitment. If DLT is load-bearing in the current pipelines, the port is materially larger than the $185,000 modelled and the whole case needs re-running.

05

On platform costs alone, the migration cost dominates

$233,000 of one-time programme against $47,000–$135,000 a year of saving. On the conservative scenario it never repays inside five years, and on the base case it takes until month 44. This is not a quick win and should not be sold as one. Two things change it decisively: landing in the best case, where existing F64 headroom absorbs production and no capacity uplift is needed, and timing the cutover to the Databricks renewal. Gate 1 resolves the first for $58,000; the second is a diary decision.

The business is renting a second analytics platform to serve dashboards, on a tenant where it has already bought and committed to a platform that serves dashboards, and that second platform is two-thirds idle.
